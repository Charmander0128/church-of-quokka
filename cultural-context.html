Cultural Context and Boundaries

Important: This page provides limited, source-based context. It does not claim that Quokkism, the Church of Quokka or any of its invented teachings are part of Noongar tradition.

Wadjemup and Whadjuk Noongar heritage

Wadjemup / Rottnest Island is a place of deep significance to the Whadjuk Noongar people. Official island sources describe long occupation before rising sea levels separated the island from the mainland, and identify Wadjemup as a place connected with spirits, memory and ceremony.

The island is also a place of grief and truth-telling because Aboriginal men and boys from across Western Australia were imprisoned there. Any cheerful tourism story about the island sits beside this continuing history and should not erase it.

The animal

The animal commonly called the quokka is Setonix brachyurus. Rottnest Island sources also give the Noongar form kwoka. The species is endemic to Western Australia and listed as Vulnerable under Australia’s EPBC framework.

Boundary of evidence: The sources below support statements about Wadjemup, language, history and conservation. They do not establish an Indigenous “quokka religion,” and this project makes no such claim.

Respectful practice

The Noongar Boodjar Language Cultural Aboriginal Corporation asks researchers and organisations to follow consultation protocols, including approaching the appropriate Elder or community organisation. The Church of Quokka will not reproduce restricted cultural knowledge, invent Aboriginal testimony or attribute its fictional material to a living community.
