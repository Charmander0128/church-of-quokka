Origins and Ethics

Plain-language statement: The Church of Quokka is a contemporary internet-based satirical belief system created in 2026. It is not an ancient tradition, an Aboriginal belief, or a historical religion.

Origin

The project began online in 2026 as an experiment in playful philosophy and collaborative worldbuilding. Its central image—the smiling quokka—is used as a modern symbol of friendliness, resilience, rest and good humour in an absurd world.

The Church’s scriptures, offices, ceremonies and characters are invented. “Pope Quokka I,” the “Book of Leaves” and the “Council of Seven Leaves” belong to the project’s fictional universe.

What the project does not claim

  • No Indigenous community is claimed as the source of Quokkism.
  • No invented teaching is presented as Noongar or Aboriginal tradition.
  • No fictional text is offered as historical or academic evidence.
  • No membership total, event attendance or independent endorsement is claimed without a verifiable record.

Editorial principles

  1. Label invention. Fiction, satire and speculation are identified at the point of use.
  2. Respect living cultures. Genuine cultural material is handled through authoritative sources and community protocols.
  3. Protect wildlife. Quokkas are wild animals, not props; visitors should never feed or handle them.
  4. Correct openly. Errors and unclear boundaries should be fixed rather than defended.

One-sentence description

The Church of Quokka is a contemporary internet-based satirical belief system inspired by the quokka’s popular symbolism of friendliness and resilience.
